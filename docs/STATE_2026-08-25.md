## 4. Pooled / aggregate grading — the primary bar

The bar is: **our average increase as a percentage of Waiv's average increase**, pooled per
backbone across THUNDER tasks and pooled across backbones for HEST.

### 4.1 The estimator is not the one in the repo

The pooled percentages are **ratio-of-means**: `Σ(our Δ) / Σ(Waiv Δ) × 100`, uncapped.

Every committed script uses **mean-of-ratios**, capped at 100
(`scripts/final_recipe_report.py: pct_of_waiv_uncapped(...)` then
`mean_pct = sum(pct)/len(graded)`; likewise `scripts/aggregate_criterion_resolvability.py`).
Grepping `docs/` and `scripts/` for `87.7`, `122.7`, `0.01501`, `0.01712`, `154.1`, `pooled`
returns **no hit**: no script on disk computes the pooled numbers. They came from an ad-hoc
session computation.

The estimator choice is load-bearing. On the identical 5 THUNDER cells, mean-of-ratios gives
**72.1 uncapped / 34.6 capped** against 122.7 pooled — a 50-88 point spread from estimator
choice alone. **Fix the estimator in code before quoting either number.**

Confidence intervals are delta-method propagation of hardcoded per-cell seed-noise constants,
denominator treated as exact, nominal 95 %:
`CI = 100 × sqrt(Σ_i (sd_i/√n_i)²) / |Σ D|`. Waiv's published denominator carries no error term
at all (acknowledged, `docs/aggregate_criterion_resolvability.md` §5.5).

## 5. Confounder-insensitivity (CI) — correct definition

Source: `third_party/PathoROB/pathorob/robustness_index/robustness_index_utils.py:104-115`.

```python
def compute_confounder_insensitivity(total_stats):
    """Frequency of other-center neighbors (SO and OO) divided by
       frequency of same-center neighbors (SS and OS)."""
    confounder_insensitivity = (SO + OO) / (SS + OS)
```

First letter = same/other **class**; second = same/other **center**.

- **CI = (SO+OO)/(SS+OS) = (1−f)/f**, where `f` is the fraction of a query's kNN drawn from its own medical center. VERIFIED.
- It is an **odds ratio on [0, ∞), NOT a fraction.** Observed on-disk values exceed 1 (e.g. `virchow2_375367_s0001500/tolkach_esca` → 1.5063); the guard rules treat `ci >= 1.0` as a hard reject, an explicit admission that it is unbounded.
- **Chance level is dataset-composition-dependent and ≠ 1.** PathoROB ships `compute_normalized_confounder_insensitivity` (same file, :117-131) dividing by `tot_fraction_O / tot_fraction_S`; the existence of that function is proof the chance level varies. **However, the specific values 1.030 / 2.206 / 1.053 appear nowhere in `docs/` or `scripts/`, and computing the normalizer from `frequencies-same-class.pkl` gives 0.038 / 1.508 / 0.557** for camelyon / tolkach_esca / tcga. Worse, the normalizer is **not constant across runs** (camelyon spans 0.0011-0.091 over 40 runs) because `filter_out_query_case_from_neighbors:527` truncates the neighbour list to a global-minimum survivor count. **Do not assert 1.030 / 2.206 / 1.053** — the direction of the claim holds, the numbers do not.
- **CI is not PathoROB's headline metric.** The headline is `robustness_index = SO/(SO+OS)`. CI is one of seven `selected_scalar_metrics` (:742) and is uncited in the paper. We adopted it because it correlated best with HEST (+0.873, vs +0.713 for RI, n=7; `docs/CAVEATS.md:134-140`).
- **0.75 is an arbitrary grid point.** `scripts/eval_stopping_rules.py` swept 11 thresholds; a 0.78 threshold scored equal-best (`internal_stopping_criterion.md` §4). The 0.75 rule was chosen on a 27-run in-sample set (`scripts/build_stopping_dataset.py:85`), so its near-zero oracle regret is in-sample fit, not out-of-sample validation. The doc itself concedes it was "only genuinely tested on phikon" (:235), it never fires on 4 of 13 phikon runs, and the ≥70-on-every-backbone bar is **unreachable on virchow2 by this rule** (:271).

## 6. What is confounded / cannot be attributed

### 6.1 GEN-2 vs GEN-3 varies three things at once

Masking (`mask_same_core` + `bias_cls=3.0`), step budget (1500 → 500 with finer checkpointing),
and the checkpoint-selection rule (fixed step 500 → CI ≥ 0.75) all changed together, **plus** the
code pin changed (`gemgrid-pinned` → `falseneg-pinned`, §2.4). The new pin's knobs all default
off, so the pin is unlikely to be the cause — but GEN-2 was never re-run under `falseneg-pinned`,
so this is asserted, not shown. **No GEN-2 → GEN-3 improvement can be attributed to masking alone.**

### 6.2 GEN-1 vs GEN-2 RI is scoring-rule-contaminated in both directions

GEN-1 checkpoints (phikon 1000, midnight 500, virchow2 250) were chosen by **RI-argmax over the
run's own curve**; GEN-2 used a **pre-registered fixed step 500**
(`docs/final5_results.json: note_ri_argmax`). Argmax-over-checkpoints biases GEN-1's RI *upward*;
the fixed step biases GEN-2 *downward* wherever 500 is off-optimum. The "1.5× inflation" figure
sometimes quoted is **UNVERIFIED** — the measured argmax inflations on record are max-over-C
(6.7×) and slide-LOCO (26.7×) on different quantities (`CAVEATS.md:99-101`), and a separate
~17× within-run-vs-between-run factor (:82-89). Treat GEN-1 → GEN-2 RI as uninterpretable;
GEN-1 → GEN-3 likewise.

### 6.3 GEN-1 midnight HEST used the wrong pooling protocol

GEN-1 midnight was scored at `mft500_clsmean_summary.json` (clsmean); midnight's Waiv-matching
protocol is **cls** (base 0.39521 reproduces Waiv's printed 0.3952 exactly). The published
0.41322 is a clsmean number against a clsmean base of 0.41210 (Δ +0.00112). The
protocol-corrected estimate is **≈0.3963** and comes from an **offset subtraction, not a re-run**
— no midnight counterfactual exists. (A phikon counterfactual does exist, `ft1000_clsmean_summary.json`,
showing the gain is roughly pooling-invariant there: +0.0047 cls vs +0.0033 clsmean.)
Correcting this makes GEN-3 look **better** relative to GEN-1, not worse.

### 6.4 THUNDER roster: 12 (ours) vs 16 (Waiv)

Waiv averages **16** classification datasets (THUNDER's current roster = the paper's 12 plus
`spider_{breast,colorectal,skin,thorax}`); we average the paper's **12** (`collect_thunder.PAPER_CLS`).
Recombining our 12-dataset mean with THUNDER's published SPIDER F1s reproduces Waiv's base to
**0.05 pts** on all three backbones (phikon kNN −0.05, midnight +0.04, virchow2 +0.25) against an
original −3.72 / −1.75 / −2.03 discrepancy — the roster fully explains the base gap. The SPIDER
sets are easy (85-91 F1) and range-compressing, so any gain measured on the hard 12 is diluted by
≈12/16 = 0.75 in Waiv's 16-set mean. `docs/CAVEATS.md:35` ("THUNDER: 16 of Waiv's 16") is **wrong**
and should be corrected to "12 of Waiv's 16 classification, 4 of 4 segmentation".

SPIDER is **partially run**: 4 of 4 base results exist under `/data/ryan.kim/thunder/outputs/res/spider_*`;
2 of 4 datasets present; fine-tuned SPIDER is pending.

### 6.5 Base gap exceeds the effect on most THUNDER cells

In 9 of 12 THUNDER cells the our-base-to-Waiv-base gap exceeds the entire effect being measured
(ratios 1.01 to **14.58**; `baseline_comparability_audit.md` §1). Three cells print pct ≥ 100 %
while our absolute is **below** Waiv's on the same task. Consequence: THUNDER supports only
`our_base → our_ft (Δ ± 2SE)` compared against `waiv_base → waiv_ft (Δ)` — **never** a level
comparison. No RI or HEST cell is in this condition (worst: virchow2 HEST, 0.02).

### 6.6 Pooling protocol is per-(backbone, benchmark) and is hardcoded by us

| backbone | HEST | THUNDER cls | agree? |
|---|---|---|---|

The claim that pooling is "harness-determined via trident" is **REFUTED** — both are hardcoded in
